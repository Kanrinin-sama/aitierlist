# Allocation refinement assessment

The implemented refinement keeps the allocation model as an API-equivalent planning proxy and makes its evidence boundaries explicit. It does not treat benchmark task units as native turns, subscription dollars as native quota, model-level measurements as native-harness measurements, or a bounded solver result as proof about every live project.

## Accepted model

Orchestrator competence is the normalized Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index alone in Absolute and For you. Both views use one row-level Intelligence Index cost and canonical decode-time reference cycle, and both hold its attempt cap at one. The resource unit represents a common benchmark-based coordination proxy; it carries no empirical orchestration-success probability. Artificial Analysis lists the current Intelligence Index evaluations and weights in its [primary methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking).

Every selected role requires all competence components used by that role. An unset floor does not admit unknown competence. Implementer and Debugger coding weights come from the task counts attached to each row's selected DeepSWE and Terminal-Bench records, so the score and resource mixture follow the actual dataset pairing rather than fixed global counts.

Terminal-Bench model data are selected atomically. A complete v4 record supplies its score, canonical resources, dataset identity, and 66-task count. Otherwise a complete v2.1 record supplies those fields and its 89-task count. A numeric zero is a valid score when the remaining record is complete; it does not trigger a fallback to an easier version. Artificial Analysis identifies Terminal-Bench v4.0 as a 66-task replacement for v2.1 in Intelligence Index v4.3 in its [version history](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking).

Retry correlation is version-qualified. DeepSWE v1.1 evidence applies only to a v1.1 row, including the exact-match difficulty adjustment. Terminal-Bench repeat evidence requires the same dataset version and an exact harness, model, and effort match. Repository Q&A borrows no rho from DeepSWE or Terminal-Bench. Missing repeat evidence still permits the measured first attempt, while extra attempts require evidence for every benchmark in a mixed workload. A user-supplied rho override explicitly enables extra attempts and supplies their common assumption. Orchestrator and Net Research remain cap one.

The proxy task graph has three stages for Focused work, eight for Standard work, and nine for Complex and Extensive work. Each class can explicitly insert one Net Research stage; all four switches are off by default. Research selection uses the existing class weights and reserves its direct model-level benchmark proxy cost and decode time on the same shared provider accounts. It does not infer research success or retry throughput. Native execution still requires the separately named harness, tools, permissions, source scope, entitlement, billing binding, and a real native-unit hold.

For AA-Omniscience, let `A` be accuracy over all questions and `H` the conditional hallucination rate among outcomes that are not correct. Define the all-question wrong and non-wrong fractions as

```text
W = H(1 - A)
R = 1 - W.
```

This agrees with Artificial Analysis's published distinction between accuracy over all questions and hallucination among cases where the model should have abstained on its [AA-Omniscience evaluation](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/omniscience). The scored Omniscience terms are `constant + w_A A - w_R W`. At fixed wrong share `W`, converting abstentions to correct answers by `delta A` increases the score by `w_A delta A`. Converting wrong answers to correct answers increases `A` and decreases `W`, so both scored terms improve. The raw `H` remains visible as a conditional diagnostic. The Focused, Standard, Complex, and Extensive research weights remain unchanged.

An optional setting, off by default, requires ordinary worker roles' selected first-attempt capability to be nondecreasing from Focused through Standard, Complex, and Extensive. It does not apply across different Net Research class mixtures or to the single conductor, and it is a policy constraint rather than a calibrated safety claim.

Conductor floor comparisons are computed on demand by resolving the full plan at available Intelligence Index thresholds. They expose the associated admission and allocation changes. The existing competence floor is the explicit user choice; there is no additional weighted-sum coefficient balancing admission against conductor quality. The explored points are not presented as a complete Pareto frontier. Optimality claims remain limited to the declared candidate set, scheduler, tolerances, and reported solver status; a feasible search-limit result retains its numerical quality bound and gap.

The current generated-orchestration contract is `aitierlist-0.2.1-team-policy-v2`. Generated files must match that policy version before dispatch; a v1 file requires regeneration. Setup-profile and ledger facts persist across that regeneration.

## Decision limits

The solver's quality bound is conditional on the forecast workload, candidate evidence, class switches, competence constraints, API-equivalent allowances, and scheduler encoded in the plan. It is not a bound on native outcome quality. Admission remains conditional on current authorization, task readiness, exact executable bindings, native account windows, shared holds, deadlines, and measured usage. Benchmark competence can rank qualified proxy routes, but it cannot establish transfer from a model evaluation harness to a different native harness.

The review reports support keeping explicit sensitivity and tradeoff reporting, class-specific task graphs, shared resource accounting, exact evidence matching, and runtime re-planning. They do not support a Poisson arrival law without observed arrivals, arbitrary research probabilities or competence thresholds, family-correlation discounts without paired error data, transferred rho values, invented conductor-overhead fractions, claimed capacity gains from a changed model, or theorem guarantees whose assumptions are absent. Such quantities remain unmodeled until task-relevant evidence identifies them.

The resulting policy is conservative about what is known: benchmark measurements determine declared proxy scores and resources; settings express visible user choices; solver bounds describe the modeled optimization; and the native ledger governs actual admission and consumption.
