# Allocation policy content snapshot

Review the allocation policy as a mathematical and operational decision system. Do not review application code, UI behavior, serialization, or implementation bugs. Treat empirical quantities as unknown unless the supplied documents give calibrated evidence.

The implemented policy admits only authorized, ready, real tasks supplied for one generation-scoped replacement set. It jointly replans that supplied set rather than globally reoptimizing every project or generation; holds outside the generation and workspace replacement scope stay fixed. It selects one persistent conductor binding across the supplied set and expands every admitted task into all mandatory and reserved conditional workflow visits, including conductor overhead. Work already started and immutable outputs remain frozen. Never-started future reservations inside the replacement scope may be jointly reassigned. Native quota is tracked by account, meter, unit, reset episode, and authoritative observation; unlike units are never summed. Unreconciled residual holds and external use remain charged until an authoritative observation covers them.

Routes must qualify before throughput optimization through an exact binding, account, executable, model, effort, harness, capability, permission, network/source, isolation, billing, freshness, role-evidence, and dependency-artifact match. Provisional benchmark transfer is limited to bounded low-consequence real work with observable acceptance. High-consequence work requires calibrated role evidence and a distinct creator/checker bundle when consequence or correlation policy triggers it. No synthetic calibration work is invented.

The allocator uses bounded integer backtracking over the full ready queue. It maintains a feasible incumbent and reports whether the constrained candidate search was exhaustive or stopped at its node limit; it reports no fabricated optimum proof, dual price, or numerical gap. The lexicographic intent is: satisfy qualifications and workflow constraints; protect high-consequence work and expiring deadlines; maximize priority-weighted useful admitted tasks; apply entitlement-weighted dominant-resource-share fairness including attributable started holds; maximize declared artifact value; then reduce normalized resource pressure and elapsed time. Idle capacity is allowed when no qualified useful task can consume it.

Scheduling uses approved absolute active intervals, task readiness and deadlines, per-visit reserve duration, one account lane, host concurrency, exclusive resources, human-attention capacity, DAG order, and the reset bound of every selected native meter episode. The implemented calendar placement conservatively serializes selected visits; it is feasible within that declared scheduler and is not claimed to solve a globally optimal parallel job-shop problem. Visits may occupy different active intervals but an individual visit is placed in one interval. Conditional visits reserve capacity until their condition becomes impossible. The receding horizon stops at observed native reset episodes; future capacity is not invented. Lifecycle changes use ledger revision compare-and-swap outside the pure optimizer.

Fairness is inspired by dominant resource fairness rather than claimed to inherit every DRF theorem. Project entitlement weights adjust accumulated dominant shares across heterogeneous native resources visible to the scoped solve, while fixed external holds affect opportunity cost without making the solve globally fair. Route-quality evidence is an eligibility gate; explicit task artifact values and priorities drive the later objective. Neither risk scores nor benchmark evidence are probabilities of team success. Correlation diversity is required only through observable differences in actual creator/checker bundles. Research visits require verified search/fetch access and a source packet. Broader global portfolio optimization and richer stochastic/job-shop models remain research aspirations, not implemented semantics.
